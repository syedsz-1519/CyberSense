# General Safe Browsing & Digital Hygiene — Knowledge Base for Chatbot Grounding

Tags: safe-browsing, general-security, malware, social-media

## Safe browsing basics
- Always check for HTTPS (padlock icon) before entering sensitive information, though note HTTPS alone does not guarantee a site is legitimate — phishing sites can also use HTTPS.
- Keep browsers and operating systems updated — security patches fix known exploited vulnerabilities.
- Be cautious with browser extensions — only install from official stores and review permissions requested.
- Avoid downloading software from unofficial/third-party sites (cracked software is a common malware distribution vector).

## Public Wi-Fi risks
Public Wi-Fi networks (cafes, airports) can be monitored by attackers on the same network (man-in-the-middle attacks) or may be fake "evil twin" hotspots mimicking a legitimate network name. Avoid accessing sensitive accounts (banking) on public Wi-Fi without a VPN.

## Social engineering beyond email
- **Vishing** (voice phishing): phone calls impersonating banks, tech support, or government agencies to extract information or pressure victims into payments.
- **Smishing** (SMS phishing): text messages with malicious links, often impersonating delivery services or banks.
- **Pretexting**: attacker fabricates a scenario (e.g. posing as IT support) to gain trust and extract information.

## Malware basics
- **Virus/worm**: self-replicating malicious code.
- **Ransomware**: encrypts victim's files and demands payment for the decryption key; best defense is regular offline backups.
- **Spyware/keyloggers**: silently monitor and record user activity, often to steal credentials.
- **Trojan**: malware disguised as legitimate software.

## Data backup basics
Follow the 3-2-1 rule: at least 3 copies of important data, on 2 different types of media, with 1 copy stored offsite/offline (protects against ransomware, hardware failure, and theft simultaneously).

## Social media privacy tips
- Review privacy settings regularly; limit what's publicly visible.
- Be cautious about oversharing information usable for security questions (pet names, schools, birthdates) since these are commonly used as password-recovery answers.
- Verify friend/connection requests, especially duplicate requests from people already connected (common impersonation tactic).
